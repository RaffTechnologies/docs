A web app gets a public https address and can serve your own domains. A private app gets a private address that your other apps call. Apps reach Raff managed databases over Raff's private network.

## How it works

### Public address

Every web app gets `https://<name>-<account id>.onraff.com`. The account id is 12 hex characters from your account. The name part comes from the app name, at most 50 characters; if it is already taken in your account, Raff adds a short suffix.

The address is fixed when the app is created. Renaming the app later changes only the name you see. HTTPS is automatic.

### Ports

Raff sends requests to the app's port: 8080 unless you set another. Your app should listen on the port in the `PORT` environment variable. Change the port in **Settings**, **Runtime**, **HTTP port**.

The platform reserves ports 8008, 8012, 8013, 8022, 9090 and 9091 on web apps and on private apps that scale to zero, so your app cannot use them.

A request to a web app can run for up to 5 minutes. Run longer work in a worker or a job.

### Private address

A private app is reached at `<name>.<namespace>.svc.cluster.local` on its port, for example `http://api.<namespace>.svc.cluster.local:8080`. The app page shows the exact address. A private app that scales to zero is reached on port 80 instead, and the app page shows that address.

### Custom domains

Web apps can serve your own domains, with an automatic HTTPS certificate at no extra cost. On the app's **Connect** tab, under **Domains**, enter the domain and press **Add domain**. Raff then shows two DNS records to create at your DNS provider:

| Type | Name | Value | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| TXT | `_raff-verify.<your domain>` | The verification value shown | Proves you own the domain |
| CNAME | Your domain | The target shown | Sends traffic to Raff |

Raff checks the records every 30 seconds, and **Check now** checks at once (5 times in 10 minutes). Once the records are right, Raff issues the certificate and the domain turns **Active**. The statuses on the way are **Waiting for DNS**, **Verifying DNS**, **DNS verified** and **Issuing certificate**.

- **Root domain** (`example.com`): most DNS providers cannot put a CNAME on it. Use an A record to the same IP as the target, or your provider's ALIAS or CNAME flattening record.
- **Cloudflare**: set the record to **DNS only** (grey cloud). A proxied record hides the CNAME and blocks the certificate.
- A domain whose DNS is still not right after 24 hours turns to **Error**. Fix the records, then press **Check now**.
- A domain can serve one app at a time. Addresses under `onraff.com` cannot be added as custom domains.
- Deleting the app frees its domains.

### Outbound traffic

Apps can connect out to the internet, except on TCP port 25 (SMTP), which is blocked. Send email through your provider's submission port (587 or 465) or its HTTP API.

### Databases

Links to Raff managed PostgreSQL databases go over Raff's private network, always with TLS. See [How apps connect to databases](/products/build/raff-apps/concepts/database-links).

## When to use it

- **The onraff.com address**: testing, and APIs that only your own code calls.
- **A custom domain**: everything your users see.
- **A private app**: services that only your other apps call, such as an internal API.

## Trade-offs

- Requests are cut after 5 minutes.
- Outbound port 25 is closed.
- The onraff.com address cannot be changed after the app is created.
