<sub>Updated October 11, 2026</sub>

Raff backs up every database every night at 02:00 UTC. You can also take a backup at any time, restore a backup into a new database while the original keeps running, or overwrite the database with an older state. PostgreSQL and MySQL on paid plans restore to any second in the last 30 days.

## Before you start

- A running PostgreSQL, MySQL, Valkey or ClickHouse database. Kafka keeps copies of every topic on its brokers instead of backups.

## Take a backup now

Open the database, go to **Backups** and click **Back up now**. It appears in the list as **Manual**, first **in progress**, then ready.

## Restore a backup

<Steps>
  <Step title="Pick the backup">
    In **Backups**, find the backup by the time it was **Taken** and click **Restore**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose where">
    - **Into a new database** (recommended): a new database on the same plan, named `<name>-restored` unless you choose a name. The original is untouched. The new database is billed like any other; on monthly billing it goes through checkout.
    - **Overwrite this one**: replaces the live data and takes the database offline for a few minutes. Raff takes a safety snapshot first. Type the database name to confirm.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Restore">
    Click **Restore to new database** or **Restore in place**. A new database is ready in about a minute.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Restore to a point in time (PostgreSQL, MySQL)

<Steps>
  <Step title="Pick the moment">
    In **Restore to a point in time**, use **Rewind to** (**20 minutes ago**, **1 hour ago**, **Yesterday**), the slider, or type a time in **Restore to**. Times are in your local time.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose where and restore">
    Choose **Into a new database** or **Overwrite this one**, then click **Restore**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

You can restore to any second since the oldest backup kept, shown as **Restore to any second since...** at the top of the tab.

## Free databases

Free databases are backed up every night and can be restored over themselves. Restoring into a new database and point-in-time restore come with paid plans.
