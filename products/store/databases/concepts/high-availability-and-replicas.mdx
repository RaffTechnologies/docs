<sub>Updated November 17, 2026</sub>

High availability keeps extra copies of the database on separate servers and switches to one automatically when the primary fails. Your apps keep the same address; at most they reconnect. Without it, a database runs on one server and is down until that server is back.

## How it works

### Per engine

| Engine | High availability runs | Failover | Price |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PostgreSQL** | A primary and a standby on another server, kept in step | Automatic, usually within half a minute; the address follows the new primary | +70% |
| **MySQL** | A 3-node cluster; every node holds all data | Automatic; a proxy sends writes to a healthy node | +210% |
| **Valkey** | A primary, a standby and Sentinel | Automatic, within seconds of the primary failing | +70% |
| **ClickHouse** | Two replicas and ClickHouse Keeper | Automatic for tables using `Replicated*` engines | +70% |
| **Kafka** | 3 brokers; every topic copied to all three | Automatic; a broker can fail without losing messages | +140% |

The percentage is added to the plan price. It is not available on the free plan.

### Read replicas (PostgreSQL)

A read replica is one more copy of a PostgreSQL database, kept in sync. Each costs the same as the plan.

A database with at least one replica gets a **read address**. Queries sent there are spread across the replicas, and a write is refused with PostgreSQL's own message (`cannot execute INSERT in a read-only transaction`). Send reports, dashboards and read-heavy pages there, and keep everything that writes on the normal address.

| From | Read address |
|---|---|
| The internet | `<database_id>-ro.public.db.raffusercloud.com`, port 5432 |
| Your VPC | The private hostname, port 5433 |

- A replica can be a moment behind the primary: a row you just wrote may not be on the read address yet.
- The read address is direct, not pooled. Each connection uses a connection on a replica.
- High availability alone gives no read address: its standby is there to take over. With replicas, the standby answers read queries too.
- Adding the first replica, or removing the last one, reconnects open connections once.

The **Connect** tab shows the read address with your values and how far the replicas are behind.

### Turning it on and off

Turn high availability on or off at any time in **Settings**, or choose it at create. Turning it on adds the copies without losing data; turning it off removes them. The database keeps running through both. One exception: a PostgreSQL database created before November 17, 2026 may restart once when high availability is turned on, so its standby can be placed on another server. See [Resize a database](/products/store/databases/quickstart-guides/resize-a-database).

MySQL is the exception: choose high availability when you create the database. A MySQL database without it runs as one server and cannot add it later: create a new MySQL database with high availability and copy the data into it, for example with `mysqldump`.

MySQL high availability is available on plans up to 2 vCPU and has limited room. When a plan has no room, the create page shows **High availability** disabled with a short note, and no payment is taken: pick a smaller plan, or create the database without it.

## When to use it

- Production databases your customers depend on.
- Anything where minutes of downtime during a server failure cost more than the extra price.

Development and staging usually run fine on a single node.

## Trade-offs

- **PostgreSQL writes wait for the standby.** The primary confirms a write to your app only after the standby has it on disk, so a failover loses no confirmed write. Each write takes a few milliseconds longer for that: in our test about 8 ms instead of 3 ms. For writes where speed matters more, your app can skip the wait per transaction with `SET LOCAL synchronous_commit = local`.
- **While the standby itself is down or catching up**, the primary keeps accepting writes alone instead of stopping. Until the standby is back in step, those writes are protected by backups and point-in-time restore, not by the standby.
- **Valkey confirms a write just before the standby has it.** A write confirmed in the last instant before the primary fails can be lost in the failover.
- During a failover, open connections drop; apps that retry reconnect to the same address.
- High availability protects against a server failing, not against deleting data by mistake. Backups cover that.
